This project explores the relationship between socioeconomic and demographic factors and the spread of COVID-19 across counties in Maryland during 2021. Using county-level COVID-19 data from the Johns Hopkins University database alongside demographic and economic indicators from the American Community Survey, the analysis examines how factors such as population size, median household income, and median age relate to COVID-19 case distribution. The project incorporates data cleaning, statistical analysis, choropleth mapping, correlation analysis, and clustering techniques in R to identify geographic and socioeconomic disparities in infection rates across Maryland counties. Findings suggest that more densely populated and lower-income counties experienced disproportionately higher COVID-19 case burdens, highlighting the importance of integrating socioeconomic indicators into public health planning and response strategies.
